Destiny Laboratory

Primary US Microgravity Research Laboratory

The scientific centerpiece of the US segment. Inside Destiny, microgravity removes the masking effects of Earth's gravity, enabling researchers to study fluid dynamics, combustion physics, and materials science.

Breakthrough Science Conducted

Houses the Cold Atom Lab (CAL), chilling atoms to a billionth of a degree above absolute zero to create exotic fifth states of matter (Bose-Einstein condensates). Also home to protein crystal growth experiments accelerating cancer drug discovery.

MICROGRAVITY SCIENCE & ENGINEERING SYSTEMS

How the Space Station Operates

The ISS is not merely an orbital habitat; it is the most complex engineering achievement in human history—combining closed-loop ecosystems, quantum physics laboratories, and 120-kilowatt power networks.

🔬

Microgravity Physics & Quantum Wonders

On Earth, gravity causes sedimentation, convection, and buoyancy that mask subtle atomic physics. Inside the ISS, researchers grow pure macromolecular protein crystals for pharmaceutical breakthroughs, study flame behavior without buoyant convection, and produce Bose-Einstein condensates in the Cold Atom Lab at temperatures colder than the depths of space.

💧

98% Closed-Loop Life Support (ECLSS)

Because launching water into orbit costs thousands of dollars per kilogram, the Environmental Control and Life Support System recycles 98% of all moisture aboard the station. The Water Recovery System purifies astronaut sweat, breath condensation, and urine through catalytic oxidizers and vacuum distillation, delivering water cleaner than municipal taps on Earth.

🧬

Human Biology & The Proving Ground for Mars

Long-duration spaceflight causes astronauts to lose 1% to 1.5% of bone mineral density per month without rigorous exercise. Through the NASA Twin Study and daily 2.5-hour workouts using the ARED resistive exercise machine, the ISS serves as humanity’s living medical laboratory, proving the protocols needed to keep crews alive on multi-year journeys to Mars.

⚡

120 kW Solar Grid & Ammonia Cooling

Over one acre of photovoltaic arrays track the sun continuously across 16 orbital sunrises every 24 hours. Because the vacuum of space has no air to absorb heat, the station cannot use fans to cool down. Instead, giant accordion ammonia radiators circulate heat away from high-powered electronics and crew quarters, dumping excess thermal energy into the void via infrared radiation.
